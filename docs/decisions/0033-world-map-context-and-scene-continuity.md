# 0033 — World map context and scene continuity

Owner request, 2026-10-26: faint country borders on the international map; capitals and major cities that fade according to zoom without crowding; repair the apparent map failure on navigation and train selection showing a flight route. Follow-up reports identify duplicate origin/destination markers and request two or three dot-density transitions while zooming. Further visual feedback requires fewer dots and a dim, lingering mouse trail instead of an immediate cursor spotlight.

The international map uses bundled, attributed country geometry and a small geographic context gazetteer. Context labels are decorative, read-only and geographically anchored. They never imply ticket availability or add cities to the search catalog. A strict label budget and collision exclusion give selected origin/destination and stop facts priority. Names fade with the actual displayed camera scale and at viewport edges. This narrowly supersedes decision 0032's restriction on general city labels; no endpoint editing or additional map panels return.

Both travel maps crossfade between four progressively finer dot levels as the camera zooms, producing three successive density changes. A bounded land mesh is triangulated once; GLSL evaluates stable geographic lattices and zoom changes only uniforms. Domestic geography remains Iran-only without context labels or country outlines. Selected markers and route geometry do not fade with the dot levels. Country borders remain subordinate to the dots and selected route. All authored colors use semantic theme tokens. The shared renderer clock and reduced-motion/fallback rules continue to apply.

Trackpad pinch (Ctrl+wheel) changes map zoom inside the map, matching the existing two-pointer pinch controls. Ordinary wheel input continues to scroll the page. Retired scene callbacks cannot change zoom. No visible zoom toolbar is added.

Dot spacing remains airy through the density transitions: defer each finer lattice until the previous level has spread sufficiently on screen. Pointer movement deposits a bounded, geographically anchored light trail with a soft onset and gradual decay. Passing back over a dot cannot accumulate an intense hotspot. The trail is substantially dimmer than selected endpoints and route light, and major-city dots use the same treatment. Reduced motion clears the trail and leaves the static ambient geography. These are presentation changes only; the map remains read-only.

The duplicate endpoints were two misaligned drawing layers: in the owner's Chrome window, the canvas was at x=-15px while the label layer began at x=0. A fixed `inset:0` canvas with `width:100vw` overconstrained RTL layout when a scrollbar appeared. The canvas now shares the fixed label layer's containing-box size. Native regression checks reserve a scrollbar gutter and compare both layers at multiple viewport heights; one origin and one destination remain mounted through navigation.

Editing a flight search to train previously left old flight results selectable; selecting one restored its flight route beneath the train form. Previous results remain readable, but their pin, selection, and referral actions are now invalid while the form differs from the searched query. A synchronous intent guard also fences callbacks retained from before the edit. A fresh matching train comparison can select and display its actual rail itinerary. This applies consistently to other trip edits and does not mutate old server evidence.

The close international view also exposed the coarse world grid as an almost-empty map. Progressive local dot levels and subdued borders preserve geographic context. Verification must distinguish that visual failure from an actual WebGL exception: capture page errors, shader/context state, route selection, camera alignment, and navigation restoration rather than assuming a cause from appearance alone.
